# Paper look, website structure

The site is typeset as a journal article, but beginners, who are most of its visitors, found pages written as papers hard to read and navigate. So we kept everything visual (the sheet on the desk, page breaks, running heads, the folio, AHA captions, the article-type tab) and changed only what the reader has to work through.

Each section keeps the paper's section name in bold navy capitals under the rule ("METHODS"), exactly as a journal sets it, and adds the plain question a student has as the heading beneath it ("How RC Works"). The capitals carry the joke; the plain heading carries the meaning. A first version set the section name small at the end of the rule, but it was too faint for readers to notice the joke. A Plain Language Summary replaces the structured abstract. Key Words, datelines and Abbreviations boxes are gone.

## Considered options

- **Keep the paper structure and add a reader's guide** in the margins. Rejected because it adds words to explain words, on pages that were already too dense.
- **Two layers**, with a plain version on top and the full paper below. Rejected because it doubles the length.

## Consequences

Do not drop the plain heading under a section name, or let "Introduction", "Methods", "Results" or "Discussion" stand alone as the heading. Do not restore the structured abstract. The paper is the look; the order and the wording follow what a first-time reader wants to know.
